# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Young, casual or newcomer ice-hockey fans (roughly under 35, Swiss-based) who open the Spengler Cup match page on their phone, check the score and leave. They may be at the rink, in a bar, on a commute or catching up after the game. They are not stats people. Source: our audience research (Spengler Cup Event Report 2025, challenge kickoff).

## Product Purpose
"Call the play" sits on the tournament's match page (ticker tab and a pop-up over the match footage). It picks six real moments from the game, asks one plain question per moment, then shows what the WiseHockey tracking data says and why. Success: a visitor who came for the score plays all six moments and understands each one, without knowing hockey beforehand.

## Brand Personality
Clear, confident, sporty: like a TV broadcast graphic. Plain words a newcomer understands, bold numbers, no hedging. It belongs to the Spengler Cup site, so it uses the site's own look, not a separate app identity.

## Anti-references
- A stats dashboard only analysts enjoy (ThinkSport's own warning).
- Game-show gimmicks: confetti, streaks, points, mascots.
- Hockey jargon in the interface ("big chance", "doorstep", "screening").
- Anything that looks like an app bolted onto the page instead of part of the site.

## Design Principles
- Understandable over good-looking: if a newcomer has to guess, rewrite it.
- One question, one tap, one answer, then the reasons one at a time.
- Show where every number comes from.
- Belong to the host page: Spengler Cup colours, type and tone.
- The clip leads; text sits over it, not beside it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text), real buttons with visible focus, an aria-live region for the reveal, team never shown by colour alone (labels and a key). Phone first at 375 px, and the pop-up must fit a laptop screen without scrolling.
